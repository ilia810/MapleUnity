The source and the normal C# solver use feet coordinates in pixels, positive Y downward, with velocities in pixels per 8 ms tick. The public Unity Player/view use center positions in world units, positive Y upward, and velocities in units per second. For horizontal velocity, pixels/tick = units/second × 100 × 0.008; vertical conversion also flips sign. The rendered feet remain half the player height below the public root.

## Remaining failures and limitations

The older test suite is not uniformly trustworthy, and not all its failures are obsolete. Examples of conflicting fixtures include treating ten world units as ten pixels, expecting movement during input polling, replacing direct movement intent with an all-false input provider, and expecting EditMode construction/destruction to execute PlayMode lifecycle callbacks. Skill fixtures also calculate incompatible job IDs, and some drop tests depend on random 50% drops.

The last full run, from the previous actor-layer milestone, had 152 failures. That full suite was not rerun during the monster port; the user asked to defer those tests. All 48 new terrain/boundary/import-order checks pass. The left/right world-input tests now pass. The randomized item-drop test passed this time after failing in the prior run; that system was not changed or fixed. Two existing fixtures were corrected: a valid spawn uses world center coordinates above its authored floor, and slope walking supplies its source foothold connection and checks the source pre-integration contact height. Existing movement fixtures still contain 60 Hz, walking-speed, airborne-acceleration, immediate-input, and reversed-Y assumptions. Remaining failures are visible; they have not been disabled or treated as proof that every remaining system works.

The attack movement pass also ran the broader `WorldRecoveryTests` fixture while checking portal timing. Its two monster-spawn assertions still expect exact ground Y before source settling (`MapLoaded_IsRaisedBeforeNewMonsterViews`, `MonsterGroundHeight_DoesNotUseSpawnX`). They remain unchanged and deferred. The focused command includes its passing portal check, and the new rewrite tests cover attack/portal timing independently.

Real gaps remain:

- Conveyor transport, remaining buff types, and passives outside the implemented source registry. Normal movement reads current Speed/Jump values each tick; unregistered passives and percentage buffs remain separate.
- Double/flash jump and unported legacy skills still use approximations. Ground-monster motion and knockback now have C++ trace coverage; flying/jumping monsters, random/aggro AI, monster skills, and network control remain incomplete.
- Full equipment/action layering and source asset coverage remain incomplete. HeavenClient's default shirt 1042399 is absent in the available Character.nx; default pants 1060026 are present and render. The character remains bare-chested until a real top is equipped; the practice White Undershirt now provides that path.
- Player, NPC, and ground-monster ordering now follows source stage layers. Drops now use those bands; reactors and remote-character integration remain unfinished. Same-category NPC ties have no source Y sort (HeavenClient uses unordered actor sets); exact overlap between NPCs is not a multiplayer ordering guarantee.
- Sound and other NX providers still contain disabled loads or placeholders. Skill metadata, the first warrior skills, Energy Bolt and Magic Claw are active. Item metadata/icons/recovery and mob stats/art are active. Equipped melee now follows the source stat, accuracy, critical and direct booster rules. The source contact-defense heuristic and local unarmed practice remain simplified; remaining passive/percentage buffs, other magic spells, additional ranged skills and server authority remain pending; broader loot tables, job/equipment growth, ranged monster skills, and the full combat/skill pipeline remain unfinished.
- The local loot/Luna shop/save loop is playable. Other NPC/shop flows, quests, AP/SP/job progression and the complete UI remain unfinished. Networking and multiplayer are deferred by request.
